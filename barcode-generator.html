Complete Retail Knowledge

Barcode Standards & Retail Label Sizing Guide

Everything you need to know about Indian and global barcode formats, HSN labeling, thermal roll printers, and scanning precision.

Which Format Should I Use?

Choosing the correct barcode symbology guarantees instant scanner recognition across POS systems and handheld readers:

  • Code 128: Best for departmental stores, apparel, MSME inventory, and custom serial numbers. Supports uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and symbols.
  • EAN-13: The global supermarket and FMCG standard. Requires exactly 12 data digits + 1 checksum digit (assigned by GS1 India).
  • UPC-A: Standard 12-digit retail barcode used across North America and global exports.
  • Code 39: High-tolerance industrial barcode used in electronics, automotive, and military tracking.

Popular Sticker Dimensions

Label printers support various continuous rolls and die-cut sticker media. Common industry standards include:

  • 50mm × 25mm (2" × 1"): The gold standard for retail MRP tags, FMCG shelf stickers, and grocery products.
  • 38mm × 25mm: Ideal for jewelry tags, optical frames, cosmetics, and compact hardware parts.
  • 50mm × 35mm: Preferred by garment and fashion brands with room for Brand, Size, HSN, and MRP.
  • 100mm × 150mm (4" × 6"): Standard logistics and shipping label for Amazon, Flipkart, Shiprocket, and BlueDart.

Ensuring 100% Scannability

Follow these best practices to ensure laser and 2D image scanners read your labels on the first attempt:

  • Maintain Quiet Zones: Leave at least 2mm of white space on the left and right margins of the barcode bars.
  • High Contrast: Always print crisp black bars on a pure white background. Avoid textured or colored backgrounds behind bars.
  • Thermal Printer DPI: For 203 DPI thermal printers, use Bar Width 2 or 3 to avoid sub-pixel dithering and bar distortion.
  • Test Scan: Always test-scan one sample label with your phone or handheld barcode gun before printing bulk batches.
